Fly Circuit Identifiability

How each training run is assembled inside flyvis, and the chain of evidence the study is built to deliver.

The model, as it trains today

A movie is rendered onto the fly's hexagonal eye, drives the photoreceptors, propagates through the full connectome of the optic lobe as a recurrent network of leaky neurons, and 34 of the 65 cell types are read out to predict optic flow. Both arms are this same network; they differ only in the two boxes below the equations.

How a run is built

Everything biological comes from flyvis unchanged. flyvis_seam.py only decides which classes flyvis instantiates, so both arms share one substrate and differ in exactly two places: the synapse signs and the time-constant dynamics.

  • flyvis, unchanged
  • shared substrate, identical in both arms
  • treatment, differs between arms
  • project tooling
What the seam changes
Softplus magnitudes, the exact integrator and the τ clamp in both arms; Dale signs and CfC gates in arm B. Dale flips 10 of 604 sign groups (8,590 edges).
What stays flyvis
Connectome, stimulus rendering, decoder, loss, optimizer, schedule and checkpoints are flyvis 1.2.0 code paths, so any difference between arms is ours.
How it is proven
verify_seam checks the built tensors, not the config labels. Eight deliberately reinserted bugs were each caught.

What the study is trying to show

Retrained flyvis models reach the same task error but not the same mechanism. The bet is that biological constraints narrow the space of solutions enough that different seeds converge on one circuit mechanism — without paying for it in task error.

The mechanism result, measured 19 Sep 2026 on the paper's own 50 models with the pre-registered fingerprint: the same T4c cell is fed by different inputs in different seeds. Mi1 excitation is the one constant (about a fifth of the input mass in nearly every model); 19 of 50 models have no Mi9 input, 11 none from Mi4, 17 none from Tm3, and two are driven mainly by lateral T4a or by Tm3. Even the overall current scale spans 7–120 A·s. Kill criterion 1 is settled; kill criterion 4 in PLAN.md still applies: do not start Stage 2 underpowered. Simulating the primary test on this real cloud of fingerprints says 30 seeds per arm are needed to detect a halving of the mechanism variance with 80 % power (24 with the pre-registration's synthetic generator, 12 with a Gaussian fit); the budget buys 8 per arm, which only detects a four-fold reduction. A 60,000-step run of both arms (20 Sep 2026, 7.7 CU, checkpoints on Drive) then closed the last shortcut: the validation loss sits at 1207 ± 1 from step 3,000 to 60,000, and the T4c input fingerprint, measured at all 62 checkpoints, moves 0.03 from its starting point — a fifth of the trained models' own spread, and not toward them. At initialization T4c is dominated by TmY15 inhibition; the trained models are dominated by Mi1 excitation. Nothing of that reorganization has begun by a quarter of the schedule, so a run cannot be stopped early and still have a mechanism to compare. What remains to be shown is that the current training recipe reproduces the trained models at all: one stock run to 250,000 steps (≈ 24 CU) would settle it, and say where in the schedule the mechanism forms.

What actually makes a run cheaper

Measured on Colab, 19 Sep 2026, arm B at extent 15, batch 4, 19 frames, on mock Sintel. One iteration on the T4 splits as: backward 34%, optimizer step and activity penalty 30%, network forward 19%, data loading 15%, decoder and loss 3%. The GPU is busy 82% of the time and launches 12,455 kernels per step, nearly all of them small gathers, scatters and elementwise passes over the 1.5-million-edge tensors.

Best measured setup
L4 with 4 concurrent seeds: ≈ 15 CU per full run, against 28 CU on a single T4 and 24 CU on an A100 running 8 seeds. The budget then buys ≈ 16 runs instead of 8. The A100's network forward pass is no faster than the L4's — only its backward is — so its 4.4× hourly price never pays back.
What would close the gap
102 runs fit in 242 CU only if training also shortens to roughly 40,000 steps. The learning curve says that is not available: both arms move from 1212.6 to ≈ 1207 in 10,000 steps, and fully trained flyvis models score ≈ 1124 under the same loss, so the whole learnable range is ≈ 7 % and 10k steps cover 6 % of it. Unmodified flyvis on the same horizon follows the same curve, and a learning rate 5–20× higher changes nothing but the noise. Shortening needs a different kind of change — or a different endpoint than the loss.
Still open
A run is 8–15 h even then, and Colab Pro has no background execution, so the resume path has to work. flyvis 1.2.0's own is broken.
